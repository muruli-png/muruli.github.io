Serials Control Workflow

Document No. LIB/SER/SOP/01  ·  Version 1.0
Covers journals, magazines, newspapers, and continuations — print and electronic.
Download the Word version (.docx) ↓

Purpose

This procedure defines the workflow for controlling serials in the library — from subscription and renewal decisions through check-in, claiming, binding, and withdrawal. Serials control covers the full lifecycle of a title, distinct from the one-time acquisition of a monograph.

Scope

  • Print journals, magazines, and newspapers (subscribed and gratis/exchange)
  • E-journals and journal packages/consortia subscriptions (e.g. e-ShodhSindhu, UGC-CARE listed titles)
  • Continuations and annuals/yearbooks treated as serials
  • Bound volumes arising from print serial holdings

Objectives

  1. Ensure uninterrupted, timely availability of current issues to users.
  2. Maintain accurate, up-to-date holdings records for every title.
  3. Detect and recover missing issues promptly through systematic claiming.
  4. Control renewal costs and avoid lapsed or duplicate subscriptions.
  5. Preserve completed volumes through timely binding and stack maintenance.

General workflow

1Title selection / subscription decision

  • Compile new-title recommendations and the renewal list annually, with usage data where available.
  • Obtain approval of the subscription list and budget from the Librarian/Library Committee.

2Subscription placement / renewal

  • Place the order or renewal through an approved agent, publisher, or consortium.
  • Record subscription details: title, ISSN, frequency, vendor, subscription period, price, and expected first/next issue.

3Prediction of expected issues

  • Based on frequency (weekly/monthly/quarterly, etc.), prepare an expected-issue schedule (kardex card or its digital equivalent).

4Check-in of issues

  • On receipt, verify each issue against the expected schedule (title, volume, issue number, date).
  • Stamp, date-stamp, and record receipt immediately.

5Claiming missing issues

  • If an expected issue does not arrive within a grace period, generate and send a claim to the vendor/publisher.
  • Track claim status and escalate or request a replacement/credit if unresolved.

6Display and circulation of current issues

  • Display current issues on the periodicals rack; apply in-house/short-loan rules.
  • Route select journals to subscribing departments/faculty where a routing list is maintained.

7Holdings recording

  • Update the holdings statement for each title (volumes/years held, gaps) after every issue or at defined intervals.

8Binding

  • Withdraw back-issues once a volume is complete; verify completeness against the holdings record.
  • Send for binding per the binding schedule; maintain a binding register.
  • Re-accession and re-shelve bound volumes in the bound-journals stack.

9Renewal review and discontinuation

  • Before each renewal cycle, review usage, cost-per-use, and faculty feedback.
  • Discontinue or replace low-use/high-cost titles per the Collection Development Policy, with Committee approval.

10Record-keeping and reporting

  • Maintain the subscription register, kardex/check-in records, claim log, and binding register.
  • Generate periodic reports on subscription expenditure, claim status, and holdings gaps.
