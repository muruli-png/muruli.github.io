Acquisition Workflow

Document No. LIB/ACQ/SOP/01  ·  Version 1.0
Covers print and electronic resources, from selection through payment and handover to cataloguing.
Download the Word version (.docx) ↓

Purpose

This procedure defines the step-by-step workflow for the acquisition of library materials — print and electronic — from identification of need to final accessioning and payment. It establishes a uniform, auditable, and efficient process for selection, ordering, receiving, and record-keeping of library resources.

Scope

  • Books (print and e-books)
  • Journals and periodicals (print and e-journals, including consortia subscriptions)
  • Databases and other electronic resources
  • Non-book materials (CDs/DVDs, maps, theses, standards, etc.)
  • Gratis, gift, and exchange materials (recording and accessioning only)

Objectives

  1. Ensure timely and cost-effective acquisition of resources in line with the Collection Development Policy.
  2. Avoid duplication of resources already available or on order.
  3. Maintain transparent, auditable financial and procedural records.
  4. Ensure compliance with institutional purchase and financial rules (GFR, GeM, tender norms, etc.).
  5. Reduce turnaround time between requisition and shelf-availability.

General workflow

The following sequential steps apply irrespective of the library management system in use.

1Identification of need / selection

  • Faculty, students, or librarians recommend titles via requisition forms, book exhibitions, publisher catalogues, or online recommendation forms.
  • Selection is guided by the Collection Development Policy (subject coverage, curriculum relevance, budget ceiling).

2Verification and de-duplication

  • Check the OPAC/catalogue to confirm the title is not already held.
  • Check the on-order file/database to avoid duplicate ordering.
  • Verify bibliographic details (ISBN, edition, publisher, price) from a standard source.

3Requisition and approval

  • Prepare a consolidated indent/requisition list with the budget head indicated.
  • Route for approval to the Librarian and, for high-value or policy items, the Library Committee.
  • Obtain a budget/fund-availability certificate from Accounts.

4Vendor selection and order placement

  • Select a vendor from the approved/empanelled list, or invite quotations as per financial rules (GeM/tender, where applicable).
  • Generate and dispatch a Purchase Order with a unique order number, specifying title, quantity, price, discount, and delivery terms.
  • Record the order in the acquisition/on-order register.

5Order follow-up

  • Track pending orders periodically (monthly/quarterly).
  • Send reminders for delayed supply; cancel and re-order if unreasonably delayed.

6Receiving and verification

  • Physically verify each consignment against the PO and the supplier's invoice/packing list.
  • Record shortages, damages, or wrong supply and take it up with the vendor for replacement or credit.
  • Stamp the ownership/property stamp on accepted material.

7Accessioning

  • Assign accession number(s) and enter details in the Accession Register (or its digital equivalent).
  • Update the on-order register to mark the order as received.

8Invoice processing and payment

  • Match the invoice with the PO and Goods Received Note (three-way matching).
  • Forward the passed invoice to Accounts/Finance for payment within the stipulated period.
  • File payment records and update the budget expenditure ledger.

9Handover to technical processing

  • Transfer accessioned items to the Cataloguing/Classification section for further processing and shelving.

10Record-keeping and reporting

  • Maintain accession registers, vendor files, and budget utilization statements.
  • Generate periodic acquisition and expenditure reports for the Library Committee/administration.
