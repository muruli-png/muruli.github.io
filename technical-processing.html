Technical Processing (Cataloguing) Workflow

Document No. LIB/TP/SOP/01  ·  Version 1.0
Covers cataloguing, classification, authority control, and physical processing.
Download the Word version (.docx) ↓

Purpose

This procedure defines the workflow for technical processing of library materials — cataloguing, classification, authority control, and physical processing — from receipt of an accessioned item to its readiness for shelving and discovery in the OPAC.

Scope

  • Newly accessioned books and non-book material received from Acquisition
  • Retrospective conversion / recataloguing of legacy collections
  • Gift, exchange, and donated material after accessioning
  • Authority records for names, subjects, and series

Objectives

  1. Create accurate, standards-compliant bibliographic records for discovery and retrieval.
  2. Apply a consistent classification scheme so related material is shelved together.
  3. Maintain authority control so headings remain consistent across the catalogue.
  4. Physically prepare items (labels, barcodes, security strips) for circulation without delay.
  5. Minimise the time between accessioning and shelf-availability.

General workflow

1Receipt from Acquisition

  • Receive accessioned items with the accession number, invoice reference, and fund/source details.
  • Verify the item and accession details match before processing begins.

2Copy cataloguing search

  • Search external bibliographic utilities/union catalogues (WorldCat, other OPACs via Z39.50/SRU, National Union Catalogue) for an existing matching record.
  • If found, import it as the basis for local cataloguing.

3Original cataloguing

  • Where no copy exists, create a new bibliographic record following AACR2/RDA and MARC21 encoding.
  • Record all mandatory fields: title, statement of responsibility, edition, publication details, physical description, series, and notes.

4Subject analysis and classification

  • Assign subject headings (LCSH, Sears) reflecting the item's content.
  • Assign a classification number per the adopted scheme (DDC/UDC/CC) and derive the call number.

5Authority control

  • Check name/series headings against the authority file; create a new authority record if none exists.
  • Ensure heading consistency across all records referencing the same entity.

6Quality check

  • A second cataloguer or the Section In-charge verifies completeness, tagging accuracy, and classification before finalising.

7Physical processing

  • Affix the call-number label, barcode/RFID tag, ownership stamp, and date-due slip/security strip.
  • Attach a jacket/lamination where local policy requires it.

8Record finalisation and OPAC release

  • Mark the bibliographic and item record as complete so it becomes searchable in the OPAC.
  • Update the item status to available for circulation.

9Shelving

  • Hand over the processed item to the stacks/circulation section for shelving in call-number sequence.

10Record-keeping, statistics, and recataloguing

  • Maintain a cataloguing statistics register (items catalogued, backlog, pending items).
  • Periodically review and recatalogue/reclassify items affected by scheme revisions or identified errors.
